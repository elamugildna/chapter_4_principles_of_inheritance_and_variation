2. Classical Genetics Ratios Summary

Monohybrid Cross (Tt x Tt)

• Phenotypic ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (3:1)

• Genotypic ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1)

• Monohybrid test cross: 1 Tall : 1 Dwarf (1:1)

Dihybrid Cross (RrYy x RrYy)

• Phenotypic ratio: 9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green (9:3:3:1)

• Genotypic ratio: 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1 (16 combinations, 9 genotypes)

• Dihybrid test cross: 1:1:1:1 (RrYy x rryy)

Incomplete Dominance (Snapdragon)

• Cross: Red (RR) x White (rr) → F1 Pink (Rr)

• Phenotypic ratio = Genotypic ratio: 1 Red : 2 Pink : 1 White (1:2:1)

Co-dominance & Multiple Alleles (ABO Blood)

• Gene I with 3 alleles: I^A, I^B, i

• 6 Genotypes: I^A I^A, I^A i, I^B I^B, I^B i, I^A I^B, ii

• 4 Phenotypes: Blood Group A, B, AB, O

3. Linkage, Recombination & Sex Determination

T.H. Morgan's Drosophila Dihybrid Crosses:

• Cross A (Body colour & eye colour): Genes yellow (y) and white (w) are tightly linked → 98.7% Parental types and 1.3% Recombinants.

• Cross B (Eye colour & wing size): Genes white (w) and miniature wing (m) are loosely linked → 62.8% Parental types and 37.2% Recombinants.

• Genetic Mapping: Alfred Sturtevant used recombination frequency as a measure of distance: 1% recombination = 1 map unit = 1 centimorgan (cM).

Sex Determination Systems:

• XX-XY type: Human beings and Drosophila (Male heterogamety: 50% X and 50% Y sperm).

• XX-XO type: Insects like Grasshoppers (Male heterogamety: males have autosomes + XO, females have autosomes + XX).

• ZZ-ZW type: Birds (Female heterogamety: female is ZW, male is ZZ).

• Haplodiploid system: Honeybees (Females 2n = 32 developed from fertilized eggs; Males n = 16 developed via parthenogenesis; males produce sperms by mitosis and have no father/sons, but grandfather/grandsons).

4. Mendelian vs Chromosomal Disorders (NCERT Core Summary)

Disorder Genetic Cause & Type Clinical Features
Haemophilia Sex-linked (X-linked) recessive; single protein in blood clotting cascade affected. Non-stop bleeding from simple cuts; transmitted from carrier female to sons.
Sickle-cell Anaemia Autosomal recessive (Chr 11); point mutation: GAG to GUG codon transversion; Glu → Val at 6th position of β-globin. HbS polymerises under low O2, causing RBC to become elongated and sickle-shaped.
Phenylketonuria (PKU) Autosomal recessive (Chr 12); deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase. Accumulation of phenylalanine & phenylpyruvic acid → mental retardation, hypopigmentation.
Thalassemia Autosomal recessive quantitative defect; α-thalassemia (HBA1, HBA2 on Chr 16), β-thalassemia (HBB on Chr 11). Reduced synthesis of α or β globin chains resulting in microcytic hypochromic anaemia.
Down's Syndrome Autosomal aneuploidy; Trisomy of chromosome 21 (2n + 1 = 47). Short stature, small round head, furrowed tongue, broad palm with crease, mental retardation.
Klinefelter's Syndrome Sex-chromosomal aneuploidy in males; 47, XXY. Overall masculine development with feminine features (gynaecomastia); sterile.
Turner's Syndrome Sex-chromosomal aneuploidy in females; 45, XO (monosomy). Sterile females with rudimentary ovaries, lack of secondary sexual characters, short stature.
